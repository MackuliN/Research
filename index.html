Publication overview

The Home Artificial Intelligence Platform (HAIP) is an owner-operated local AI engineering system and research environment. It treats authority, resource validity, knowledge state, execution, failure, and recovery as one connected evidence problem.

HAIP asks two linked questions:

  1. What evidence is required before the owner can responsibly admit, retain, or recover a local AI-assisted result?
  2. Can an AI system preserve the relationships that answer that question, rather than reducing HAIP to a list of component concepts?

The six layers are:

  • owner authority;
  • bound knowledge;
  • qualified resources;
  • bounded action;
  • independent verification; and
  • retained disposition.

A favorable result at the end of the chain cannot repair an invalid condition earlier in it. A passed test cannot repair stale authorization. A citation cannot grant permission. A guest pass cannot erase a trusted host failure. A recovered record cannot silently authorize replay.

Inside the full paper

The paper describes:

  • HAIP's public and internal working names;
  • the six connected evidence layers;
  • the action lifecycle from proposal to retained disposition;
  • how local resource, retrieval, verification, failure, and recovery evidence constrain one another;
  • a bounded observation that component recall did not guarantee whole-system comprehension; and
  • the limits of the current evidence.

The accompanying evidence and claim boundaries identify what supports each public claim, what remains private, and what this release does not establish.

Citation guidance is provided in CITATION.md.

Evidence scope

This preview draws on four completed, bounded research areas:

  • local inference and resource qualification;
  • revision-specific local knowledge retrieval and recovery;
  • a bounded repository workflow with isolated guest verification; and
  • comparative evaluation contracts with finite attempts and retained failure evidence.

The current record is a private, single-environment case study with limited task coverage and some synthetic fixtures. It does not establish general autonomous coding ability, production security, independent certification, or universal applicability.

Publication boundary

This preview intentionally excludes proprietary source, private operational records, credentials, device identities, network details, home-infrastructure topology, and instructions for deploying HAIP.
